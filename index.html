01Overview

Abstract

Video world models should predict how a scene evolves under a specified physical event, yet online post-training on their own generated rollouts often yields limited gains. We identify two missing ingredients: rollouts must provide informative alternatives for comparison, and the learning objectives must agree on the resulting update. We call these requirements contrast and consensus. We introduce COSMIC (COntrastive Sampling and Multi-objectIve Consensus), an online reinforcement learning framework that operationalizes both principles. We first construct PhysRL, 1,371 target-free image–instruction conditions that pair a visible initial state with a specified physical event. For each condition, COSMIC generates structured alternative futures using antithetic initial-noise pairs, evaluates transition correctness and visual fidelity separately, and applies Sign Conflict-Aware NFT Optimization (SCAN) to suppress samples with conflicting reward-specific advantages. At the behavior-policy anchor, we show that each retained SCAN contribution is locally compatible with both reward objectives, whereas a sign-conflicting sample admits no nonzero compatible update along its NFT direction. With only 1.35K training conditions, COSMIC improves Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B from 81.0 to 82.0 in Overall Score and from 83.9 to 86.0 in Domain Score on PAI-Bench-G. It improves all six domains while maintaining comparable visual quality, and outperforms the official 14B post-trained model by 1.0 Domain points. Our results show that self-generated futures become effective supervision when they are organized to provide both contrast among rollouts and consensus among objectives. Code, models, and datasets will be released.

06Limitations

Current Evaluation Scope

Broader I2W evaluation

COSMIC is not tied to a particular dataset; validating the framework across a broader range of Image-to-World datasets remains future work.

Larger model scales

The current evaluation focuses on the 2B backbone due to computational constraints; extending COSMIC to larger-scale models remains future work.
